# Break to Escape — Engineering Decisions

Each entry: the decision, the alternatives considered, and the reason. Decisions marked **(owner to confirm)** were made without the owner's input to keep work moving; change them freely.

## Process and repository

| # | Decision | Alternatives | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | The playable Unity source lives in `UnityProject/Assets/`; `codebase/` stays untouched as the historical first draft. | Edit `codebase/` in place. | Keeps the audit reproducible and lets the harness compare old and new. |
| D2 | The first-draft scripts sit directly in `codebase/` (there is no `codebase/original-scripts/` folder and no ZIP in the repository, although the README mentions both). They were read from there. | — | Recorded as a documentation gap (issue P3-7). |
| D3 | Work happened on the branch this session was assigned rather than a branch named `game-implementation`. **(owner to confirm)** | Create `game-implementation`. | The session's repository rules only allow the assigned branch. Rename the branch on GitHub if the name matters. |
| D4 | Unity-independent logic is in `Assets/Scripts/Core` with its own assembly definition (`noEngineReferences: true`); all other runtime scripts compile into Unity's default assembly. | Assembly definitions for every folder. | Tests need an assembly to reference; the rest stays simple for a first Unity project and automatically sees URP, TextMeshPro and the Input System. |
| D5 | Tests are written once (NUnit, the subset Unity's NUnit 3.5 supports) and run both with `dotnet test` and in Unity's Test Runner. | Separate suites. | One source of truth for test logic. |

## Economy and data

| # | Decision | Alternatives | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| D6 | `Resources/Data/economy.json` is generated by `tools/verify/generate_economy_json.py` from the spreadsheet's cached values; numbers that are not in the spreadsheet come from `tools/verify/economy_extras.json`. `codebase/economy_config.json` is superseded and not copied. | Hand-edit JSON; read the .xlsx at runtime. | Exact match with the spreadsheet, one source of truth, no spreadsheet parser in the game. |
| D7 | In-game prices are the spreadsheet's exact costs rounded to whole dollars (away from zero). Example: Punch Power level 4 → 5 costs $1,597.2 in the sheet and $1,597 in game. | Keep fractional cash. | Cash is an integer in the HUD; rounding error per purchase is < $0.50. |
| D8 | Equipment numbers (Work Gauntlets ×1.15 damage, Pneumatic Hammer ×1.25 punch speed, Rock Drill 3 layers deep) are kept from the first draft. Repair costs ($2,500 hammer, $3,000 conveyor, $20,000 drill) are new starting guesses. **(owner to confirm)** | — | The design names the machines but has no numbers. Costs sit near the planned cash at the band where each part is found. |
| D9 | Pneumatic Hammer "tighter pattern" is not implemented; the hammer only speeds up punches. | Shrink the pattern while the hammer is restored. | The design gives no rule and shrinking the pattern would cut income; recorded for tuning. |
| D10 | Store items without spreadsheet values keep the first draft's prices: dynamite pack $400 for 3, auto-punch $1,200, treasure detector $650. Headlamp levels 2 and 3 cost $500 and $1,800. **(owner to confirm)** | — | Starting guesses. |
| D11 | Treasure uses one system: special voxel types (copper, gold, geode seams) that drop high-value rubble, at about 1 in 600 rock voxels plus a six-cube seam in each hidden nook; four named "showcase" treasures are buried objects. The first draft's 1-in-128 density was lowered. | Keep both systems at the old density; buried objects only. | The pacing check shows treasure on the main path adds 1–5 % per band, so the spreadsheet's pacing still holds (`tools/verify/pacing_check.py`). |
| D12 | Dynamite blast damage is 500 (one blast clears band 6 rock, 420 HP) with a 1.75 m radius. The first draft used 15, which would not break anything past band 1. **(owner to confirm)** | — | The design says dynamite is "great for clearing hard rock". |

## World and punch

| # | Decision | Alternatives | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| D13 | The world stays 96 × 32 × 96 voxels (48 × 16 × 48 m). The design says about 100 × 50 × 100. | Resize now. | Six chunks a side is enough for the v1 map; resizing is a data change (`worldSizeVoxels`) once performance is measured. |
| D14 | Bands are laid out by direction from the Collapse Chamber: a band-1 shell (10 voxels), then west = band 2, east = bands 2→3, south = bands 3→4→5, north = bands 4→5→6. Rooms: Old Gallery west (band 2), Equipment Bay east (band 3), Pump Room south (band 4) with the fuel-cell stash beside it and the Rock Drill part beyond it in band 5, Ore Veins store north (band 5, winch cable), Crystal Grotto (band 6) and the Main Shaft at the north end. | Concentric rings (first draft). | Follows the design map; each route has a clear difficulty. The design places the Rock Drill in the Pump Room "band 5"; the Pump Room is in band 4 here and the drill part sits in a band-5 stash just south of it. |
| D15 | Punch pattern shapes are the explicit rules in `PunchPatterns.Contains` (counts 5, 9, 13, 21, 29, 37, 49, 61, 77, 97). Note: the shapes are not strictly nested (level 8 is a diamond that does not include level 7's square corners). | Radius growth. | Exact designed counts and symmetry, tested. |
| D16 | Charged punch: the charge ratio is (hold − 0.18 s) / (1.15 s − 0.18 s), clamped 0..1. Pattern bonus = floor(ratio × 2) levels (so +1 from half charge, +2 at full charge), capped at level 10. Damage × (1 + ratio). A release before 0.30 s counts as a tap. A charged punch uses one punch cooldown. | Round instead of floor; separate charge cooldown. | Simple, predictable, matches the requested +2 / ×2 limits. |
| D17 | The Rock Drill applies the full pattern to each of 3 depth layers independently. | Narrow tunnel shape for depth. | The bug was the shared counter; a narrower drill shape is a tuning choice left open. |
| D18 | Auto-punch: when owned and switched on in Settings, holding PUNCH repeats normal punches at the Punch Speed rate (no charging while it is on). | Fire continuously without holding (first draft). | The design says "punches for you while you hold the button". |
| D19 | Aim, preview, punch, dynamite placement and interaction use voxel-grid rays; physics queries remain only for rubble (vacuum, conveyor, crusher trigger). Chunk collision meshes are cooked into a second mesh buffer and swapped in, at most 2 per frame. | Bake with `Physics.BakeMesh` on a worker thread. | Simple and safe first step; `Physics.BakeMesh` is the next step if profiling shows cooking spikes. |

## Rubble, vacuum and rates

| # | Decision | Alternatives | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| D20 | Rubble caps 100 / 200 / 400 (Mid uses 200 from the 150–250 range). The pool is created at the tier cap at start-up; raising the quality tier later raises the cap only up to that pool size until the next launch. | Always prewarm 400. | Memory on low phones; the design asks for the pool to match the tier. |
| D21 | If the cap is reached and no settled cube can be swept, the new cube is not spawned and its 60 % refund is paid immediately. | Sweep an unsettled cube; drop the cash. | Never pay twice, never lose cash, never touch a cube being vacuumed or crushed. |
| D22 | Vacuum suction and blow-out share one token bucket at the Vacuum Suction rate (burst = a quarter second of suction). | Unlimited blow-out. | Keeps the model's vacuum rate meaningful in both directions. |
| D23 | Physics step per tier: 30 Hz (Low), 50 Hz (Mid), 60 Hz (High). The technical plan lists 30 Hz for Low only. **(owner to confirm)** | 60 Hz everywhere. | 30 Hz may make rubble look coarse on Low; profile on the low phone. |

## Save

| # | Decision | Alternatives | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| D24 | Save format v2: magic "BTES", format byte, CRC-32, gzip JSON. Written to `.tmp`, previous file moved to `.bak`, then `.tmp` moved into place; loading tries main → tmp → bak. | `File.Replace`. | `File.Replace` support on Android IL2CPP was not confirmed; the move sequence uses only basic file calls and is tested. |
| D25 | Version 1 saves are migrated: cash, day, the four stored upgrade levels, auto-punch, detector, ads-removed, dynamite and broken voxels carry over. The player position, room ids and tank count are dropped (they referred to the old, wrong coordinates and the tank contents were never stored). | Try to keep them. | They would put the player in rock or create cubes from nothing. |
| D26 | JSON (a small built-in reader/writer) instead of `JsonUtility`. | `JsonUtility`. | The same code runs in tests outside Unity; `JsonUtility` cannot serialize dictionaries or nested lists cleanly. |

| # | Decision | Alternatives | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| D27 | Canvas reference resolution 1688 × 780 (the 844 × 390 wireframe at 2×), match height. Every button is at least 96 canvas units (48 dp at 2×). | 1920 × 1080. | Wireframe pixel positions map directly. |
| D28 | The Company Store has two tabs: UPGRADES (the wireframe's 7 cards + ad card) and SUPPLIES (dynamite, headlamp, detector, auto-punch, free dynamite, three repairs, Remove Ads, Restore Purchases). | D30 | URP renderer: keep **Forward** (not Forward+) for the prototype. The shader also declares the Forward+ / cluster light-loop keywords, but that path is unverified. | — | Fewer unknowns for the first compile. |

| D32 | Quit to Menu saves and closes the app; there is no separate menu scene yet. | Build a menu scene. | Out of scope for the first playable build. |

## Monetization and services

| # | Decision | Alternatives | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| D34 | Remove Ads owners receive the rewards without watching, at a gentler rate: double cash every 20 minutes instead of 10, 2 free dynamite crates per session instead of 3. **(owner to confirm)** | Same rate. | The design says "at a gentler rate" without numbers. |
| D35 | The no-ads grace period counts total play time of the run (first 5 minutes), not each session. | Per session. | "No ads during the first 5 minutes" reads as the player's first experience. |
| D36 | SDK adapters target the Google Mobile Ads plug-in (with UMP consent) and Unity IAP 4.x.
